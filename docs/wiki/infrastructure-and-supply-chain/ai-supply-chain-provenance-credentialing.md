## Security Impact

- Threat or control area: weak provenance can let unverified model artifacts, datasets, generated code, MCP packages, plugins, or deployment components enter AI systems without traceable trust evidence.
- Affected boundary: AI supply-chain credentialing, artifact provenance, verification workflows, attestation records, and security-review evidence for AI system components.
- Exploit or incident status: official funding notice; no exploit, incident, product deployment, or adopted standard was reported by the source.
- Mitigation state: treat provenance credentialing as an assurance requirement, but require concrete implementation evidence before relying on any named platform.
- Confidence: medium for government interest in AI supply-chain provenance; low for operational control details until award, architecture, or implementation material is available.
- Residual risk: provenance programs can become label-only controls if credentials are not tied to reproducible builds, signed artifacts, identity, revocation, and downstream policy enforcement.

## Control Implications

- Record provenance evidence for AI models, datasets, generated artifacts, MCP servers, plugins, and runtime images.
- Require credential issuers, signing keys, verification policy, revocation path, and audit retention to be explicit.
- Keep funding, proposal, standard, and deployed-control evidence distinct in governance records.
- Link provenance checks to release gates so unverifiable artifacts cannot silently enter production or evaluation environments.
